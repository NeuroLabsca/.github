## 1. Evidence of the pain (for and against)

**Real owner voices (Mike Holt forum, electricians):**

- "When I first started in business, people would call me at all hours. More than once I was awakened by my business cell phone ringing. That ended when I started using an on-line virtual auto attendant." https://forums.mikeholt.com/threads/what-limits-do-you-place-on-incoming-client-calls.150923
- "I turned off the ringer on my phone... My VM outgoing message tells them that they will get much faster service if they hang up and send a text." (same thread)
- "All of our good customers have my personal cell. 24/7 if needed... Anyone that calls the shop after hours gets the message machine with the on call electricians phone number for emergencies. I have only gotten a handful of business related non emergency related calls after hours." (same thread; **against**: after-hours non-urgent volume can be small)
- "I have two phones one for work and one personal... when the phone goes off at 3:00 in the morning it can be a little hard trying to figure out which one is ringing." https://forums.mikeholt.com/goto/post?id=1835434
- "I use one number, one phone... two phones would be out of the question." / "Huge fan of google voice." (same thread; **against**: many solo operators are content with one number or free Google Voice)
- On-call burden: "$60 and time for any calls... That comes up to $0.46 an hour if I don't get any calls!" https://forums.mikeholt.com/threads/on-call-serive.53649/. HVAC techs report $20/week, $8/week, or $50/call for carrying the phone (https://hvac-talk.com/vbb/threads/1135201-On-call-pay).

**Second-line app pain (Capterra reviews of OpenPhone/Quo):** "Sometimes I don't get notifications for calls"; "calls i missed don't even show up until I open the app"; "Does not integrate well with Jobber yet"; "AI responder is overpriced" (https://www.capterra.com/p/199031/OpenPhone/reviews/). Top complaint pattern: email-only support.

**Owner groups ask the question.** Blue Collar Millionaire thread "What answering service the plumbers use in this group?" (https://www.facebook.com/groups/bluecollarmillionaire/posts/1379639160836555/); PlumbingZone "After hours emergency service" thread (https://www.plumbingzone.com/threads/after-hours-emergency-service.17762/page-2). Bodies not fetchable.

**Secondhand Reddit synthesis (vendor-written):** Ciela claims r/smallbusiness consensus is AI receptionists are "worth it as a safety net that never misses a call; not worth it as a full replacement" (https://ciela.ai/blogs/are-ai-receptionists-worth-it-reddit).

**Trade press:** Plumber Magazine (Sept 2026) lists four after-hours approaches: rotating on-call tech, human answering service, voicemail, AI voice agent "but needs human escalation paths" (https://www.plumbermag.com/online_exclusives/2026/09/is-your-approach-to-after-hours-calls-hurting-business).

**Net read:** the "always on" pain is real and old, but owners have cheap workarounds. The unsolved part is the triage.

## 2. Quantification

**Missed calls:**
- "62% unanswered" traces to a 411 Locals mystery-shop of only 85 businesses (https://www.ambscallcenter.com/blog/business-phone-stats). Marketing.
- Invoca: home services miss ~27% of inbound calls; ~$1,200 avg value per missed sales call (https://www.invoca.com/blog/how-much-missed-sales-calls-cost-home-services-businesses).
- CallRail 2025 (1.1M tracked leads): home-services missed-call rate **14%**.

**After-hours share:**
- ServiceTitan State of the Trades (2025 platform data, residential HVAC): only **14.1%** of inbound calls outside Mon to Fri 8 to 5 at the June peak, **9.8%** in October; concentrated 5 to 9 pm (https://www.servicetitan.com/toolbox/state-of-the-trades/trends/hvac-summer-after-hours-call-spike).
- Vendor claims of 42 to 73% after hours are unsourced (Contractor In Charge, NextPhone).
- Property management: ~30% of maintenance requests outside business hours (https://oxmaint.com/industries/property-management/reducing-emergency-maintenance-calls-property).
- Locksmith: 7 pm is the daily peak per Keytek UK (https://www.keytek.co.uk/blog/the-7pm-discovery-why-the-most-common-hour-for-lockout-calls-is-right-after-the-commute/).

**Emergency job values (2026):** emergency plumber ~$170/hr, $150 to 250 after-hours fee (https://www.angi.com/articles/emergency-plumber-cost.htm); emergency HVAC $140 to 600/hr; locksmith lockout $125 to 295 after hours (https://homeguide.com/costs/locksmith-cost); towing $125 to 375. HVAC replacement leads $3,500+ (Invoca). 81% of homeowners had a home-repair emergency in the past year (HomeServe 2025).

**Two phones / second line:** GoDaddy found 86% of small businesses use one smartphone for both (https://techcrunch.com/2017/08/15/godaddy-smartline/). No credible 2025 to 26 adoption rate for second-line apps found.

**What they pay for answering today:**
- Ruby: $250/50 min, $395/100, $720/200, $1,725/500; recommends limiting after-hours transfers (https://rubyhelpcenter.helpjuice.com/en_US/answering-hours/afterhours-options).
- Smith.ai: $300/30 calls, $810/90, $2,100/300 (https://smith.ai/pricing/receptionists).
- AnswerConnect: $350/200 min (https://vida.io/blog/answerconnect-pricing). Towing live answering $500 to 1,000+/mo. Property-management answering $175 to 470/mo. Dental $200 to 800/mo, $800 to 1,500 with emergency routing.
- AI tier: Rosie $49 to 149; Jobber Receptionist **$29/mo for 30 conversations, unlimited on Plus** (https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/receptionistpowered-by-jobber-ai/); CallsAround $79 to 299.

## 4. Do owners trust AI to decide urgency?

- Bluevine/Centiment, Apr 2026, n=942 SMB owners: 78% "don't fully trust AI to handle low-level tasks without oversight" (https://www.kesq.com/stacker-small-business/2026/07/15/3-4-of-small-businesses-dont-trust-ai-for-basic-tasks/).
- Simply Business, n=1,047: 86% say a human "exit ramp" is important; accuracy is the top barrier.
- LEX Reception/OnePoll, n=6,000, Sept 2026: 89% prefer a real person calling a law firm; trust that AI handles the inquiry correctly is 32%. Metrigy Q1 2026: 59.1% will give an AI voice agent a chance if human escalation exists.

**How incumbents do escalation (mostly keywords):** Jobber keyword triggers → SMS or transfer; CallsAround keywords + custom phrases → sequential ring chain with AMD and press-1; Rosie "AI-driven analysis" with zero verified reviews; Goodcall takes a message; Ruby human with "press 3 if urgent"; ServiceTitan live escalation to CSR; Housecall Pro CSR AI transfers to "a number of your choice," texts in alpha. Industry guidance: "a missed emergency is worse than a false alarm, but constant false alarms defeat the purpose" (https://voicefleet.ai/blog/ai-receptionist-escalation-rules-2026-06-17). **No documented, independently-verified false-negative incident found**, which reflects how new and vendor-dominated the category is.

## 5. Willingness to pay and churn

- Anchors: human services $250 to 720/mo; AI $29 to 299/mo; second-line apps $10 to 20/user. Churn trigger for human services is variable billing ("Ruby was great at 30 calls/month, now 150+ and my bill is insane").
- Realistic band for an owner-operator: $49 to 149/mo flat. Below $29 you're under Jobber's bundled add-on.
- SMB SaaS churn 3 to 7% monthly (https://www.kalungi.com/blog/saas-churn-rate-benchmarks). Phone-app churn drivers: missed notifications, dropped calls, email-only support, porting delays, poor Jobber/HCP integration. For this product the killer churn event is one missed real emergency.
- Seasonality: after-hours HVAC share rises ~45% June vs October; expect seasonal cancels unless annual billing.

## Skeptic's bottom line

**For:** always-on pain is real; emergency jobs are worth $300 to 3,500; owners pay $250 to 720/mo to humans who mostly take messages; incumbents' urgency detection is keyword matching; no one bundles second-line + triage + digest cleanly.

**Against:** (1) ServiceTitan's real data says after-hours is 10 to 14% of HVAC volume. (2) Jobber ships calls + texts + keyword escalation for $29/mo inside the tool 200k pros already use. (3) Emergencies arrive by voice. (4) 78% of SMB owners won't trust AI without oversight, and one missed flood is a churn disaster. (5) Owners already have free workarounds. (6) Zero independent evidence of AI-triage failure or success in the trades.

**What would change the picture:** 15 to 20 interviews with 1 to 5 truck plumbers who currently pay Ruby/Smith/AnswerConnect. If fewer than a third say yes at $99+/mo, kill.
