Why this exists

The community platform model works for the person running it.

Open-ended memberships need you to stay. So they add levels, points, streaks, a leaderboard, a course drip and a weekly "value" call, and the product becomes the retention machine rather than your business.

A cohort is the opposite shape. It has a fixed roster, a fixed calendar and a finish line. When it ends, it ends. Nobody upsells you into a mastermind, and there is no tier above the one you paid for.

What we don't do

  • Leaderboards and points
  • Levels that unlock content
  • Monthly membership
  • Drip-fed course modules
  • Affiliate links and referral tiers
  • A guru at the front of the room
  • Open enrolment

What we do

  • Twelve people, chosen by application
  • Two live working sessions a week
  • One build review per person, every week
  • A shipping target on the calendar
  • One flat price, paid once
  • A facilitator who is building alongside you
  • A last day

How it runs

Two calls a week. The rest is build time.

Tuesday session
90 minutes. One topic from the program, taught by whoever in the room has actually done it, then applied to your own product on the call. No slides longer than ten minutes.
Thursday review
90 minutes. Four people show what they shipped since Tuesday. The other eight break it. You get a review every third week and see everyone else's the rest of the time.
Between calls
One group chat, text only, on Signal. Async help, links, screenshots. It's archived on the last day of the cohort.
Facilitator
One person runs the cohort and is shipping their own AI product on the same schedule. Their week ten demo is graded like everyone else's.
Attendance
You commit to both calls each week. Miss three and you are asked to leave, with a pro-rated refund. Small rooms only work if people show up.
After
An alumni list, a shared directory of who built what, and first look at the next cohort's applications if you want to help review them. No ongoing fee.

Fit

Who this is for, and who it isn't.

Good fit

  • You have a product idea that uses a model, and you can already write or vibe-code enough to get a prototype up.
  • You've been in a Skool, Circle or Discord "community" and left because nothing shipped.
  • You can make two 90-minute calls a week, in Eastern time, for ten weeks.
  • You want the finish line more than the network.

Not a fit

  • You want to learn to code from zero. That is a different ten weeks.
  • You are selling an AI course, agency or "done for you" offer and want leads. The roster is not a lead list.
  • You're looking for accountability without a deliverable. The deliverable is the accountability.
  • You need certainty about the outcome. Some products won't work, and week 10 will say so.

Questions

Why only twelve?

Because Thursday reviews have to be real. Twelve people at four reviews a week means everyone gets a full 20-minute review every third week. At thirty people it becomes a webinar.

What if I don't get in?

You get a reply saying why, in a sentence or two. Usually it's timing, stage, or the calls not fitting your timezone. You can reapply to the next cohort without rewriting the form.

Which models and tools do you use?

Whatever your product needs. The program is model-agnostic. Week 3 and week 7 are about evaluating and hardening your model calls, which matters regardless of vendor. You bring your own API keys and pay your own token bill.

Is there a course I can buy instead?

No. The notes from each Tuesday go to the cohort and to alumni. They aren't for sale on their own because the notes without the room are the thing we're trying not to sell you.

Can I join late or take it self-paced?

No. Everyone starts on 5 Oct and finishes on 11 Dec. If you miss the window, the next cohort opens for applications in January.
