## 1. Ideal customer profile and exclusions

**Beachhead ICP:** Independent Ontario mortgage brokerage, roughly 3 to 25 licensed agents and brokers, licensed and operating before October 11, 2024, owner or principal broker acting as compliance officer, no internal AML staff, no effectiveness review completed or booked, not under FINTRAC examination.

**Exclusions for the field test, and why**

| Excluded | Reason |
|---|---|
| Brokerages under a national network or franchise brand | Possible centrally arranged tools or reviewers; unknown until asked. Include only if the fit check shows no arranged reviewer. |
| Brokerages with 100 or more licensees | Likely internal compliance staff and existing providers; FSRA examination tier ([FSRA 2025-26 plan](https://www.fsrao.ca/industry/mortgage-brokering/regulatory-framework/supervision/mortgage-brokering-sector-supervision-plan-2025-26)). |
| Mortgage administrators | Different obligations and investor-facing scrutiny. Second wave. |
| Private lenders and MICs | Different record set. Second wave via CAPL context. |
| Quebec entities | No bilingual specialist capacity. |
| Entities under FINTRAC examination or with a notice of violation | Escalation-only path. |
| Entities licensed after October 11, 2024 | Different first-review timing question. Counsel to confirm. |

## 2. Twelve-question fit assessment (non-sensitive)

No borrower information. No file uploads. Answers route the prospect; they are not a compliance opinion.

1. What type of mortgage business are you? (Brokerage / Administrator / Lender / More than one / Not sure)
2. Which province is your head office in? (Ontario / Other, specify)
3. Was your business licensed and operating before October 11, 2024? (Yes / No / Not sure)
4. Approximately how many licensed agents and brokers work under your brokerage? (1 to 2 / 3 to 10 / 11 to 25 / 26 to 100 / More than 100)
5. Has your business completed a two-year effectiveness review of its AML compliance program? (Completed / Booked with a reviewer / Not started / Not sure what this is)
6. If not completed, do you already have a reviewer arranged, for example through your network or an AML firm? (Yes / No / Not sure)
7. Who acts as your compliance officer? (Owner or principal broker / Another staff member / External provider / Not appointed)
8. Which of these written documents does your business currently have? (Policies and procedures / Risk assessment / Training program and plan / Written plan for the two-year review / None / Not sure)
9. When did your team last complete documented AML training? (Within 12 months / 12 to 24 months / Longer or never / Not sure)
10. Roughly how many mortgage files did your business arrange since October 11, 2024? (Under 50 / 50 to 200 / 200 to 500 / More than 500 / Not sure)
11. Has your business received a FINTRAC examination notice, notice of violation, or compliance agreement? (Yes / No) [Yes routes immediately to the examination procedure; no other questions are asked.]
12. When would you want an effectiveness review completed? (Before October 11, 2026 / By December 2026 / Early 2027 / No target)

Routing (internal, not shown to prospect):

- Q11 = Yes: stop, escalation script, no sale.
- Q5 = Completed or Booked, or Q6 = Yes: thank, log as "covered", ask one question about what they wish had been easier.
- Q1 = Administrator or Lender, or Q2 = Other, or Q4 = 26+: log as "second wave".
- Otherwise: qualified for a discovery call.

## 3. Principal-broker discovery script (20 minutes)

Opening (2 minutes)
"Thanks for the time. I'm [Name] from Neurolabs. We're a Canadian product and UX firm. We are exploring whether small brokerages want help getting organized for the FINTRAC two-year effectiveness review, with the review itself done by an independent AML practitioner. I'm not an AML professional and this isn't advice. I'd like to understand your situation. Is 20 minutes still fine?"

Situation (6 minutes)
- "How did the October 2024 FINTRAC obligations land for your team? What did you set up?"
- "Who handles compliance day to day? How much of your own time does it take?"
- "Have you heard that FINTRAC expects first reviews completed before October 11, 2026? What have you done about the review so far?"
- "If you've talked to anyone about doing the review, what did they quote and how did that go?"

Pain (5 minutes)
- "If a reviewer asked you tomorrow for your policies, risk assessment, training records, and a list of files since October 2024, how long would it take to pull together?"
- "What worries you more: cost, time, or not knowing whether what you have is enough?"
- "Does your network or software provider help with any of this?"

Offer reaction (5 minutes)
- "Here's what we're testing. Neurolabs organizes your documents, an evidence list, file counts, and the interview schedule in about two weeks for a fixed CAD 2,500. An independent AML practitioner, engaged by you directly, does the testing and writes the review, quoted separately by them. Neurolabs does not perform the review. What's your honest reaction?"
- "Would you rather pay one firm for everything, even if it costs more, or two clear invoices?"
- "Is a 48-hour Evidence Readiness Map for around CAD 500, credited toward the sprint, something you'd buy first?"

Close (2 minutes)
- "If this were available next week, would you want a proposal, a call with the practitioner, or nothing right now?"
- "Who else in your brokerage would need to agree?"
- "May I follow up by email on [date]?"

Never ask about specific borrowers, transactions, or suspicious activity. If the prospect volunteers such details, redirect: "That's a question for the reviewer. I'll note it as one to raise with them."

## 5. Objection-handling guide

| Objection | Response | Never say |
|---|---|---|
| "Our network handles this." | "Good. Does the network arrange the reviewer, or provide templates and training only? The review is per legal entity, so it's worth confirming who is doing yours." | "Networks don't cover you." |
| "I'll do it myself, the rules allow it." | "They do. FINTRAC's best practice is that the reviewer not be directly involved in the program, and the review has to test effectiveness, not just confirm documents exist. If you self-review, the readiness map still helps you document it. Your call." | "Self-review is non-compliant." |
| "A consultant quoted CAD 3,000 for the whole thing." | "That can be the right choice. Our sprint is for owners who don't have time to pull the evidence together and want a clear record of what exists and what's missing before the reviewer starts. If you're already organized, you may not need us." | "They'll do a bad job." |
| "Why two invoices?" | "So it's clear who is responsible for what. The practitioner signs the review and carries the professional judgment. We do the organizing. Mixing the two would blur that." | "It's just paperwork." |
| "Is Neurolabs FINTRAC approved?" | "No. FINTRAC does not approve providers. Anyone who says they are approved is wrong." | Anything implying approval |
| "Will this guarantee we pass an exam?" | "No one can guarantee that. A documented, tested review is what examiners look for; the reviewer's report is the deliverable that matters." | "You'll be fine." |
| "We already got a FINTRAC notice." | "Then stop here. Preserve everything as is, don't change any records, and talk to the practitioner or counsel first. We don't do readiness work during an examination." | Any readiness pitch |
| "It's too late for October 11." | "Possibly for the date FINTRAC named. The obligation doesn't expire. A late but real review is a different position than no review. The reviewer can tell you how to document timing." | "You'll be fined." |
| "Can you just write our policies?" | "No. If we wrote them, the reviewer's independence would be questioned. We can index what you have and track what the reviewer says needs to change." | "Sure." |

## 6. AML-specialist partnership brief

**Purpose:** invite an experienced Canadian AML practitioner to be the named reviewer for small mortgage brokerages coordinated by Neurolabs.

**What Neurolabs does:** marketing, fit checks, intake coordination, evidence indexing (metadata only), file-universe counts, interview scheduling, report formatting to your content, 30-day memo formatting, remediation tracking. Neurolabs never sees borrower records, never selects samples, never states conclusions, and never authors program documents.

**What you do:** applicability, scope, methodology, sampling, testing, interviews, findings, missed-STR analysis, conclusions, report and sign-off, and the professional relationship with the client under your own engagement letter and invoice.

**Commercial model:** separate contracts and invoices. No fee-splitting. If any referral consideration is ever proposed, it is disclosed to the client in writing. Neurolabs' value to you: organized evidence on day one, fewer chasing hours, predictable scheduling, and a repeatable small-brokerage format.

**Asks:** (1) a written quote and turnaround for a 3 to 25 agent brokerage with under 200 files in the period; (2) confirmation of capacity for October to December 2026; (3) your minimum evidence list so the index matches your method; (4) permission to name you, with approval rights over every use.

**Not offered:** exclusivity, volume guarantees, or any representation about your services beyond your approved wording.
