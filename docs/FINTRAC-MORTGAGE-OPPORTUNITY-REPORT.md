## 1. Executive summary

**The regulatory trigger is real, but it is a guidance expectation, not a filing deadline, and it is five weeks away.** FINTRAC's own mortgage-sector training video states that mortgage businesses subject to the Act on October 11, 2024 "should have their first two-year review completed before October 11, 2026" ([FINTRAC video, modified 2025-02-20](https://fintrac-canafe.canada.ca/training-formation/mortgage-hypotheque/video-eng)). Nothing is filed with FINTRAC. The review is documented, retained, and reported in writing to a senior officer within 30 days ([PCMLTFR s. 156(3) and (4)](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/SOR-2002-184/section-156.html)). FINTRAC sees it only during an examination.

**Enforcement of the review obligation is now visible and cheap to trigger.** In December 2025 FINTRAC penalized an Ottawa real estate brokerage $33,000 where the prescribed two-year review was the sole violation ([FINTRAC release, 2026-07-09](https://fintrac-canafe.canada.ca/new-neuf/nr/2026-07-09-2-eng)). No mortgage-sector penalty has been published yet ([AMP list, modified 2026-07-09](https://fintrac-canafe.canada.ca/pen/4-eng)). Penalty maximums rose sharply for violations after March 26, 2026 ([FINTRAC AMP changes, modified 2026-05-06](https://fintrac-canafe.canada.ca/pen/3-eng)).

**The market is large enough for a beachhead.** Ontario had 1,193 licensed mortgage brokerages and 242 administrators in 2024 per FSRA ([FSRA 2024-25 supervision plan](https://www.fsrao.ca/industry/mortgage-brokering/regulatory-framework/supervision/mortgage-brokering-sector-supervision-plan-2024-25)). Size distribution by agent count is not published in a form we could retrieve, so the share of 3 to 25 agent independents is an inference.

**Competition is real but not productized for small brokerages.** Ten providers were examined. None publishes a mortgage-specific readiness offer with a price. Full-service AML firms bundle readiness into their reviews. Networks (Centum, DLC) provide tools and training, and at least one national firm says it works with large mortgage networks. The observed white space is a fixed-price, fixed-scope evidence-readiness and coordination layer for small independents, sold with a named external reviewer. It is defensible only if it shortens and cheapens the specialist's work.

**Economics are marginal at the proposed prices.** A CAD 2,500 sprint clears the CAD 1,500 contribution gate only if Neurolabs delivery stays under roughly 10 to 12 hours and the specialist bills the client directly. Reported review prices for comparable small reporting entities range from CAD 3,000 to 16,000 ([Globe and Mail, 2026-07-22](https://www.theglobeandmail.com/real-estate/article-real-estate-anti-money-laundering-paperwork-fintrac/)), so a readiness fee plus a review fee may look expensive next to a cheap all-in review.

**Provisional recommendation: GO TO FIELD TEST, not launch.** Run the five-day test with the "before October 11" hook reframed as "overdue and undocumented is the risk", because the hook itself expires in five weeks. Do not build anything. Launch only if the field gates in section 8 are met with real evidence.

### 2.2 What the review must cover, test, document, retain, report, and remediate

All from [FINTRAC guidance](https://fintrac-canafe.canada.ca/guidance-directives/compliance-conformite/guide4/4-eng) and the [assessment manual](https://fintrac-canafe.canada.ca/guidance-directives/exam-examen/cam/cams-eng) unless noted.

- **Cover:** policies and procedures, risk assessment, ongoing training program and plan. Examiners also expect coverage of current legal requirements, current operations, and all applicable sector obligations.
- **Test:** effectiveness in practice, not just existence on paper. Examples: staff interviews, sampling client identification records, reviewing transactions for STR decisions, verifying high-risk client measures.
- **Plan and document:** rationale for focus areas, period reviewed, methods, sample sizes; date conducted, period covered, reviewer identity, results, deficiencies, action plans.
- **Retain:** documented results (s. 156(3)). Period for the review record itself is UNKNOWN (claim 20). Source records: five years (claim 19).
- **Report:** written report to a senior officer within 30 days of completion, covering findings, policy updates in the period, and implementation status (s. 156(4)).
- **Remediate:** examiners look for evidence that findings were actioned and policies updated.

### 2.3 Boundary between administrative readiness and professional AML judgment

[INFERENCE from claims 3, 8, 9, 10.] Readiness work that involves no AML judgment: building an index of what documents exist, their versions and owners; confirming presence or absence; assembling a count and ID-only list of files in the review period; scheduling interviews; formatting the specialist's report and the 30-day senior-officer memo; tracking remediation deadlines. Everything that decides applicability, scope, sampling, adequacy, suspicion, findings, or conclusions belongs to the specialist. See the [boundaries document](FINTRAC-MORTGAGE-COMPLIANCE-BOUNDARIES.md).

### 2.4 Is an external reviewer required?

**Optional in law, best practice in guidance.** s. 156(3) permits an internal or external auditor, or self-review if the entity has no auditor (claim 3). FINTRAC's impartiality best practice (claim 8) makes self-review by an owner-compliance-officer weak. Outlier, a competitor, frames the same point as preferring an external provider that did not build the program ([Outlier services page](https://www.outliercanada.com/about/services/aml-compliance-effectiveness-review-services/)). That is a commercial statement, not law.

### 2.5 Unresolved issues for counsel or an experienced reviewer

1. Legal weight of the "before October 11, 2026" expectation and how FINTRAC treats a first review completed in, say, December 2026.
2. Whether readiness support by a third party affects the reviewer's impartiality in FINTRAC's view (claim 22).
3. Post-March-2026 penalty amounts for a missing or inadequate prescribed review (claim 14).
4. Retention period for review documentation (claim 20).
5. Person versus entity treatment for sole-proprietor licensees (claim 21).
6. Whether a reviewer who later helps remediate can review the next cycle.

### 3.3 Beachhead recommendation

**Independent Ontario brokerages with 3 to 25 licensees, owner or principal broker as compliance officer, no review completed or booked.** Reasons: highest impartiality problem (owner cannot self-review credibly), least internal capacity, single decision-maker, and a public licence register to build lists from. Exclusions explained above.

**Ontario first, Canada-wide brand:** Ontario has the largest verified population and a single regulator with a public register. British Columbia is mid-transition to a new Mortgage Services Act with a reported in-force date of October 13, 2026 (secondary sources only; [Fasken](https://www.fasken.com/en/knowledge/2025/08/bill-29-a-new-landscape-for-mortgage-services-in-british-columbia) could not be retrieved), which adds noise. Alberta licenses over 3,700 individual brokers per RECA search snippets, but brokerage entity counts were not verified. [UNKNOWN]

**Quebec: defer.** Mortgage brokerage is supervised by the AMF, a public register exists ([AMF registers](https://lautorite.qc.ca/en/general-public/registers)), but entity counts were not retrieved, and Neurolabs has no bilingual specialist capacity. Serving Quebec brokerages in English only would be a delivery and language-law risk. [INFERENCE]

### 3.4 Method for a 100-entity validation list (no contact, no borrower data)

1. Use FSRA's public licence search ("Mortgage Brokerages, Administrators, Mortgage Agents, and Mortgage Brokers Licensed in Ontario") to list brokerages by name and licence number. The open-data bulk download is currently "not yet available for download because it is under review" ([FSRA open data page](https://www.fsrao.ca/about-fsra/corporate-reporting/open-data/licensed-mortgage-brokerages-administrators-mortgage-agents-and-mortgage-brokers)), so expect manual or page-by-page collection.
2. For each brokerage, count licensees shown as authorized under it to estimate size. Keep those showing roughly 3 to 25.
3. Exclude names carrying a national network or franchise brand.
4. Confirm the legal entity and registered office through the free public search of the [Ontario Business Registry](https://www.ontario.ca/page/ontario-business-registry) (updated 2026-08-11).
5. Confirm operation before October 11, 2024 from the licence issue date or incorporation date.
6. Record only: legal name, licence number, city, estimated licensee count, incorporation or licence year, public website, generic business phone or contact-form URL, network flag, source URLs, and collection date. No personal data beyond the publicly listed principal broker name, and no borrower data ever.
7. Store the list in the validation tracker described in the [validation assets](FINTRAC-MORTGAGE-VALIDATION-ASSETS.md).

Do not extrapolate Ontario counts to a Canada-wide total without repeating this method per provincial regulator.

## 4. Competitor findings

Full matrix with citations and evidence dates: [competitor matrix](FINTRAC-MORTGAGE-COMPETITOR-MATRIX.md).

Key findings:

- **No provider publishes a mortgage-specific readiness offer with a price.** The AML Shop, MNP, Platino, AlphaDelta, Outlier, and C&G all offer effectiveness reviews and bundle preparation inside the engagement. None lists a price for mortgage brokerages.
- **Published price anchors are thin and not mortgage-specific.** C&G's August 2026 article puts a low-complexity MSB review at CAD 5,000 to 8,000 ([C&G](https://candg.ca/insights/fintrac-independent-review-cost/)). The Globe reported a real estate brokerage quoted CAD 16,000 and negotiating to CAD 3,000 ([Globe, 2026-07-22](https://www.theglobeandmail.com/real-estate/article-real-estate-anti-money-laundering-paperwork-fintrac/)). Fintracker publishes real-estate packages at CAD 2,000 plus CAD 300 per month for 24 months for 50 or fewer agents, including "automated effectiveness reviews" ([Fintracker](https://www.fintracker.ca/additional-services/)). Whether an automated review satisfies s. 156 is a question for the specialist. [UNKNOWN]
- **Software vendors do not perform reviews.** Mortgage Automator, Centum's tool, Finmo, FINTRAC App, and Fintracking address ID verification, screening, and record-keeping. Finmo states explicitly that each brokerage remains responsible for its program ([Finmo help](https://help.finmo.ca/en/articles/9888744-fintrac-requirements-and-faq-s)).
- **Complium is not a relevant competitor.** It is an EU-based firm whose Canadian activity is MSB registration ([Complium](https://complium.com/aml-compliance/)).
- **Solo practitioners exist and compete on price.** Example: AML Review and Compliance Consultants (BC, ex-FINTRAC, real estate references) ([site](https://www.amlcompliancereview.ca/)).

**White space [INFERENCE]:** a productized, fixed-scope evidence-readiness and coordination service for small independents, paired with a named external reviewer, with a plain-language explanation of who does what. Defensibility depends on lowering the specialist's hours enough that the combined price is competitive with a cheap all-in review. If a specialist can already do a small brokerage review for CAD 3,000 with no preparation, Neurolabs adds cost, not value, unless the client is genuinely disorganized.

**Positioning test:** "Neurolabs organizes the evidence and delivery process; a named Canadian AML specialist performs the testing and reaches the professional conclusions." This is accurate. The confusion risks and recommended wording are in the [boundaries document](FINTRAC-MORTGAGE-COMPLIANCE-BOUNDARIES.md), section 5.

### 5.2 Contract structure

Recommendation: **separate contracts and invoices**, plus a written coordination and data-handling agreement between Neurolabs and the specialist. Neurolabs as prime contractor is rejected because it makes Neurolabs look like the reviewer, pulls professional liability and the review fee onto Neurolabs' insurance and balance sheet, and blurs who controls borrower data. ## Recommendation

Authorize a five-business-day, zero-build field test of the Review-Readiness Sprint for independent Ontario brokerages, with the October 11 hook reframed as an overdue-documentation risk. Do not launch a productized service yet.

## Evidence supporting it
